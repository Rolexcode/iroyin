The case for Ìròyìn

Many public complaints begin as a spoken account, but formal systems expect a polished written form. For people who naturally code-switch, that can mean translating themselves and reconstructing exact details under stress. Important facts can disappear before the complaint is even written.

Ìròyìn starts with the speaker's natural Pidgin–English or Yorùbá–English voice, with Igbo–English available as a clearly labelled preview. Sahara by Intron transcribes it, and the speaker checks exactly what the model heard. Explain makes difficult language easier to understand. Express rewrites the same thought in a chosen register. Report creates a structured, evidence-linked incident record, detects critical gaps, asks for clarification, and requires human verification before export.

Sahara is the essential first-mile speech layer, not a decorative integration. Ìròyìn elevates its code-switched transcript into a transparent, user-controlled record while keeping the original words and provenance visible.

Natural inputSpeak or upload Pidgin–English and Yorùbá–English without translating first; Igbo–English is available as an unbenchmarked preview.
Evidence, not guessesFacts retain transcript evidence; missing critical details become questions.
Reporter controlCorrect, verify, copy, or export. No complaint is submitted automatically.

Working prototype

  • Live Sahara recording and audio-upload transcription.
  • Original transcript preservation and visible reporter corrections.
  • Explain and Express modes with selected language/register.
  • Deterministic incident extraction, evidence links, missing-field clarification, and correction.
  • Explicit reporter verification, text/PDF export, and informational official-channel routing.
  • Browser-local cases with 24-hour expiry; server-side API keys; no automatic submission.

Responsible AI

Speech recognition can be wrong. Ìròyìn keeps the original transcript visible, distinguishes reporter corrections, links facts to evidence, preserves uncertainty and negation, asks instead of guessing when critical facts are missing, and requires explicit human verification. It does not decide whether an allegation is true, provide legal advice, replace emergency services, or submit reports.

Audio is sent only after the user chooses to transcribe. Report cases stay in the current browser and expire after 24 hours. The public benchmark excludes private audio, real complainant data, consent records, and API keys.

Technical execution

Ìròyìn is a Next.js 16 and React 19 application deployed on Vercel. Server-only routes send multipart audio to Sahara, map the chosen speech pair to pcm or yo, handle queued jobs, and preserve the original transcript separately from reporter corrections. Deterministic incident extraction keeps exact evidence spans, derives required fields by scenario, asks bounded clarification, and invalidates verification after correction. Complete case schemas are validated again on the server, and PDF export is blocked until the record is complete and reporter-verified.

The Python 3.11 benchmark package freezes selection, normalization, provider settings, scoring, and v1.1 provenance. It validates license/consent, human reference slots, counts, unique IDs, and SHA-256 hashes before inference. Current checks: 5 frontend tests, 10 benchmark tests, lint, TypeScript, and production build all pass.
